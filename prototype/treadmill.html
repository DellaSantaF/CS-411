Developer review notes · physical and display simplifications

This demonstration uses gravity, an idealized belt-contact impulse, and a Magnus force perpendicular to velocity. It is not calibrated to an ordinary treadmill bounce.

  • Release height: 4.5 m; rebound restitution: 0.92. Belt speeds: 6–10 m/s (default 8).
  • The aerodynamic force is amplified 12× relative to the selected baseline coefficient. Baseline lift coefficient: 0.25 at the default belt speed, varying proportionally with belt speed as an illustrative mapping.
  • Horizontal distance is shown at 2.5× the vertical scale. Playback runs at 0.55× physical time. The rendered ball's rim speed matches the rendered belt speed, using rotation frequency = belt speed / ball circumference in the drawing's units.
  • Contact is illustrated over 25 ms, with at most 4% compression. Visible spin is synchronized for teaching; the trajectory still uses the idealized solid-sphere impulse model. The tennis-ball drawing does not make this a calibrated tennis-ball model.
  • Air drag, spin decay, and deformation dynamics are omitted. Only ascent is shown; playback stops at the apex. Belt and ball animation share the same clock and pause together.

The motion model and these settings remain provisional until designer review. Details and source links are in motion-review.md.
